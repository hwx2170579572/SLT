# Scene-representation redesign: topology and interaction evidence

Search window: 2024-10-03 through 2026-10-03. This note screens recent primary work on road/lane topology, agent–map and agent–agent relations, multimodal futures, and temporal scene representations. The project’s main setting supplies structured SUMO state and an authoritative road network, so camera-based map-construction scores do not establish a policy benefit here. The scope is a candidate redesign of the critic representation `z_t` for `(s_t,a_t)` with SAC unchanged; this is a literature note, not an implementation or novelty claim.

## Search and selection

Queries used public, generic terms only: “traffic topology scene graph lane connectivity traffic signal”; “interaction scene graph agent map future trajectory”; “lane topology graph generation autonomous driving ICCV CVPR 2025”; “multi-agent behavioral topology interactive autonomous driving”; “future-aware motion forecasting agent map temporal”; “scene graph conflict zone vehicle intersection resource acquisition”; and “scene-agent-goal autonomous driving 2026”. I screened official CVF, IJCAI, NeurIPS, PMLR, publisher, and author-code sources, then followed primary PDFs/repos for the six core papers below. A few directly relevant candidates were retained as abstract/repository-level screens rather than represented as full reviews. Venue/year are taken from the proceedings or publisher pages, not inferred from arXiv dates.

### Direct cross-disciplinary conflict-resource prior

**Communication-free Distributed Control Algorithm for autonomous vehicles at intersections (CfDCA)**, *Transportation Research Part C* 180 (2025), 105309, DOI [10.1016/j.trc.2025.105309](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trc.2025.105309), available online 2025-08-28. [Publisher-author PDF](https://transportlab.sydney.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/AS-DL-MR-2025.pdf). The paper explicitly models vehicle and conflict-zone nodes, directed vehicle-to-zone requirement/acquisition edges, path-based zone sets, entry/exit times, clearance intervals, and priority ordering (§§2.1–2.2). It therefore blocks novelty claims for a resource/conflict graph or entry/clearance events alone. Its important boundary is equally clear: fixed predetermined lane paths, strict protocol-following AVs, and a deterministic distributed controller; it does not learn a multimodal critic or establish value of mode-preserving latent graphs. It reports safety/deadlock arguments and traffic-flow simulation, not SAC evaluation.

## Older novelty anchors (outside the search window)

These are not presented as recent work. **LaneGCN** already combines lane-graph relations and actor–map interactions ([author code/paper link](https://github.com/uber-research/LaneGCN)); **MTR** explicitly optimizes global intention localization and local movement refinement with motion-query modes ([NeurIPS 2022](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/2ab47c960bfee4f86dfc362f26ad066a-Abstract-Conference.html)); **QCNet** has query-centric vector scene encodings and recurrent multimodal future proposals ([CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Zhou_Query-Centric_Trajectory_Prediction_CVPR_2023_paper.html)); **GameFormer** models reciprocal future agent responses and joint prediction/planning through hierarchical game reasoning ([ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Huang_GameFormer_Game-theoretic_Modeling_and_Learning_of_Transformer-based_Interactive_Prediction_and_ICCV_2023_paper.html)). A new proposal must be distinguished from these alongside the 2024–26 papers.

## Critical review of the candidate representation

Candidate under review: for each **joint** future mode, represent each vehicle’s continuous path progress and project it to conflict-zone entry/exit or clearance events; perform actor–zone message passing along movement/resource topology; preserve the mode set until the critic reads `z_t` and ranks actions, instead of averaging modes into pairwise risk weights first.

The candidate is narrower than “two graphs plus spatiotemporal attention,” but novelty is **not established**:

1. GraphAD already gives each actor modality its own dynamic node, links modalities through synchronized minimum distance over future trajectories, and links each future proposal to static map geometry. Its graph is not merely an unordered current-distance neighbor graph. Any distinction must name the additional semantics and a measurable critic-level consequence.
2. BeTop already supervises agent-pair future topology from joint future trajectories and uses that topology to guide attention. Its topology labels encode future behavior, not just pairwise proximity. “Keep multiple future modes” or “behavioral topology” alone is insufficient differentiation.
3. CfDCA already represents conflict zones as resources, connects vehicles to required/acquired zones, and reasons about entry, exit, clearance, and priority. A learned version of those nodes/edges is not novel just because it is differentiable or embedded in attention.
4. A possible residual is a *policy/value-specific* formulation: a probability distribution over correlated joint movement modes whose mode-conditioned, path-ordered conflict-event representation is a sufficient or more learnable statistic for action value under the online observation/action contract. That remains a hypothesis, not a proven gap. It needs an explicit distinction from GraphAD’s actor-modality nodes and BeTop’s topology edge labels, and evidence that preserving modes before value readout changes action ranking beyond ordinary graph pooling, BeTop-like topology features, and independent per-zone risk heads.

Before a full study, minimum discriminating checks would be: define how joint modes and their probabilities are inferred only from available observation/history (no evaluation-time future leakage); formalize event times/uncertainty and route/movement legality; compare (a) mode-preserving actor–zone representation, (b) mode-averaged features, (c) independent-zone risk heads, and (d) a parameter/FLOP-matched generic graph-attention critic; keep the same SAC, training/evaluation protocol, reward, and action interface; then verify critic action-ranking/calibration and closed-loop task outcomes over multiple training seeds. This is a design recommendation only. The candidate is not yet validated as novel or useful.

## Evidence limits

Most recent works here solve supervised map construction, motion forecasting, or perception-plus-planning. Their reported OLS, minADE/minFDE, open-loop collision, or task-specific simulator scores do not predict performance of this project’s critic-only state representation. The one study with reactive closed-loop evidence among the core six is BeTop; MomAD and Flow Planner also report closed-loop evaluations, but their pipelines, data, planners, and metrics differ. None is a fair performance baseline for the proposed SAC experiment. Several papers use future trajectory labels during training; those labels may be valid auxiliary targets if trained from replayed sequences, but future information must never enter the policy/critic input at evaluation. Single-task scene-graph modularity is not causal attribution.
